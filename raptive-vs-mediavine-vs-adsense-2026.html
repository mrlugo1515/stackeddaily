    Raptive vs Mediavine vs AdSense in 2026: Which Pays More for Small Blogs?

    If you've started a blog and you're trying to figure out how to actually make money from display ads, the advice you'll find online is mostly either outdated or vague. Here's the straight answer: the network you should use depends almost entirely on where your traffic is right now — and the path forward is clear once you understand each network's requirements and what you'll actually earn.

    This guide breaks down Raptive, Mediavine, and Google AdSense honestly — traffic thresholds, real RPM ranges, and the exact progression strategy that makes sense for new bloggers in 2026.

    What Is RPM and Why It Matters

    RPM (Revenue Per Mille) is how much you earn per 1,000 pageviews. It's the most useful number for comparing ad networks because it accounts for how efficiently the network monetizes your existing traffic. A network with a $30 RPM earns you $30 for every 1,000 sessions, regardless of how many ads are on the page.

    RPM varies significantly by niche, audience geography, and season. Finance, business, and legal content commands higher RPMs than food or DIY. US-based traffic pays 3–5x more than traffic from developing countries. Q4 (October–December) typically earns 30–50% more than other quarters due to advertiser holiday budgets. Keep these variables in mind when you see the numbers below.

    Google AdSense: The Starting Point for New Bloggers

    AdSense is Google's self-serve ad program and the only option with no traffic minimum. You can apply as soon as your site has a few posts and legitimate traffic — Google primarily checks that your content is original, your site has clear navigation, and you're not violating their policies.

    AdSense Requirements

    • No official traffic minimum (unofficially, 100+ daily sessions helps)
    • Content must be original and comply with Google's policies
    • Site must have clear navigation and an About/Contact page
    • Must be 18+ with a valid payment method

    AdSense RPM in 2026

    Real talk: AdSense RPMs for small blogs typically land between $2–$8. In high-value niches (personal finance, insurance, legal), you might see $10–$20. But for most beginner bloggers writing about general topics, productivity, or lifestyle, expect $3–$6 RPM. That means 10,000 monthly pageviews earns you about $30–$60/month — not life-changing, but it's real money while you build traffic.

    AdSense Pros and Cons

    • Pro: No traffic minimum — start earning immediately
    • Pro: Google's demand network is massive; ads always fill
    • Pro: Auto-ads place themselves without manual configuration
    • Con: Lowest RPMs of the three networks
    • Con: Limited optimization tools compared to premium networks
    • Con: Account bans can happen without much warning or explanation

    Mediavine: The Major Upgrade at 50,000 Sessions

    Mediavine is a premium ad management company that manages your ad inventory and optimizes placements for maximum yield. The difference between AdSense and Mediavine isn't just RPM — it's that Mediavine actively works to increase your earnings through header bidding, lazy loading, and continuous placement testing.

    Mediavine Requirements (2026)

    • 50,000 sessions/month (not pageviews — sessions are unique visits)
    • Majority of traffic must be from Google Search (not social-only)
    • Content must be original and long-form (not aggregator or thin content)
    • Must use Google AdSense before applying (active AdSense account required)
    • No significant policy violations or low-quality content history

    Mediavine RPM in 2026

    Mediavine bloggers in mainstream niches typically report $15–$35 RPM. Food and recipe bloggers often see $20–$40. Finance content can hit $40–$60+. Compare that to AdSense's $3–$6 for similar traffic — the jump is substantial. A blog doing 60,000 monthly sessions might earn $90–$360/month on AdSense vs. $900–$2,100/month on Mediavine. That gap is why hitting 50,000 sessions is such a meaningful milestone.

    Mediavine Pros and Cons

    • Pro: RPMs are typically 5–8x higher than AdSense
    • Pro: Excellent publisher support and active optimization
    • Pro: Detailed dashboard with session RPM, earnings by post, and video revenue
    • Con: 50,000 session minimum is a real barrier — takes most blogs 6–18 months
    • Con: Mediavine ads can slow site speed if not configured properly
    • Con: They take roughly 25% of gross revenue (your cut is 75%)

    Raptive: Premium Network for Blogs at 100,000+ Pageviews

    Raptive (formerly AdThrive) is widely considered the top-tier display ad network for lifestyle, food, and content publishers. They're selective — they turn down a significant portion of applicants — and they focus on high-traffic sites with established audiences.

    Raptive Requirements (2026)

    • 100,000 pageviews/month (not sessions — pageviews)
    • Majority of traffic must be US, UK, Canada, Australia, or New Zealand
    • Content must be original, high-quality, and not in a prohibited niche
    • Manual review — they can and do reject sites that don't meet quality standards

    Raptive RPM in 2026

    Raptive publishers report $20–$50+ RPM depending on niche and audience quality. Food and recipe blogs at Raptive average $25–$45. Finance and business can exceed $50. Raptive also has a video ad product that can significantly boost RPM for blogs that embed video content. The difference between Raptive and Mediavine for the same traffic level is typically $5–$15 RPM — meaningful at scale, but Raptive's higher threshold means most bloggers won't qualify until they're well-established.

    Raptive Pros and Cons

    • Pro: Highest average RPMs among the three networks
    • Pro: Strong video ad monetization
    • Pro: Dedicated account managers for larger publishers
    • Con: 100,000 pageview minimum excludes most small and mid-size blogs
    • Con: Manual review means no guaranteed acceptance
    • Con: Revenue split is approximately 75/25 (publisher/network) similar to Mediavine

    RPM Comparison Table: AdSense vs Mediavine vs Raptive

    Network Traffic Minimum Typical RPM Best For Revenue Split
    Google AdSense None $2–$8 New blogs under 50k sessions 68% publisher
    Mediavine 50,000 sessions/mo $15–$35 Mid-size blogs 50k–100k 75% publisher
    Raptive 100,000 pageviews/mo $20–$50+ Established blogs 100k+ 75% publisher

    RPM ranges are averages based on mainstream niches with primarily US traffic. High-value niches (finance, legal, health) typically earn 2–3x these figures. Q4 earnings are typically 30–50% higher.

    The Smart Progression Strategy for New Bloggers

    The clearest path forward for a new blog in 2026 is a three-stage progression that matches your traffic to the right network at each stage:

    Stage 1: Start with AdSense (0 to ~50,000 sessions)

    Apply to AdSense as soon as your site has 10–15 quality posts and is getting consistent organic traffic. The RPMs are modest, but getting your AdSense account established early is actually a requirement for Mediavine — they want to see that you're already monetized with Google. Use this phase to focus almost entirely on traffic growth: publish consistently, build backlinks, and optimize existing content for search. The $30–$100/month you earn from AdSense at this stage is a bonus, not the goal.

    Stage 2: Switch to Mediavine at 50,000 Sessions

    The moment you cross 50,000 monthly sessions (check Google Analytics — sessions, not pageviews), apply to Mediavine. The application takes 2–4 weeks to process. When you're approved, you'll swap out your AdSense ads for Mediavine's code — they handle everything from there. Expect your monthly ad revenue to jump 5–8x overnight for the same traffic. This is the single most impactful move you can make in the 50,000–100,000 session range.

    Stage 3: Consider Raptive at 100,000+ Pageviews

    Once you're consistently at 100,000+ monthly pageviews, you can apply to Raptive and compare the offers. Some publishers switch; many stay with Mediavine. The RPM difference is real but not dramatic — the decision often comes down to which company's support and optimization tools feel like a better fit for your site. Don't leave Mediavine just to join Raptive for a potential $5 RPM bump unless your traffic is large enough for it to mean hundreds of dollars per month.

    Common Mistakes New Bloggers Make with Ad Networks

    • Applying to Mediavine too early: If you apply at 30,000 sessions hoping they'll accept you, they won't — and some bloggers report stricter scrutiny on reapplications. Wait until you're clearly over the threshold.
    • Ignoring niche when evaluating RPM: A food blog and a finance blog with identical traffic will earn very different amounts. Don't benchmark against bloggers in different niches.
    • Prioritizing ad revenue over traffic growth too early: At under 20,000 monthly sessions, obsessing over ad optimization is rearranging deck chairs. Focus on traffic — that multiplies everything else.
    • Running AdSense and another network simultaneously: You can't use Mediavine or Raptive alongside AdSense — they take over your ad inventory completely.

    What About Other Networks (Ezoic, Monumetric)?

    A few other networks are worth knowing about. Ezoic has no traffic minimum and uses AI to optimize ad placement — RPMs typically land between AdSense and Mediavine ($8–$20), making it a reasonable middle step if you're between 20,000–50,000 sessions and not yet Mediavine-eligible. Monumetric requires 10,000 monthly pageviews and offers slightly better RPMs than AdSense. Both are legitimate options if you're in the gap, but neither is a long-term destination — Mediavine remains the clear target for most bloggers in the 50,000+ range.

    Bottom Line: Which Network Should You Use?

    The answer is straightforward:

    • Under 50,000 sessions: Use Google AdSense. It's the only real option, and having an active account is required for Mediavine anyway.
    • 50,000–100,000 sessions: Apply to Mediavine as soon as you hit the threshold. The RPM jump is dramatic and makes a meaningful income difference.
    • 100,000+ pageviews: Apply to Raptive and compare. Many bloggers stay with Mediavine; both are strong choices at this scale.
